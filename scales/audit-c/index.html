Clinical context: when AUDIT-C is used

3-item brief version of AUDIT for quick alcohol screening in clinical settings. AUDIT-C is part of standard practice in this setting because it provides a structured, replicable assessment that can be tracked over time and compared across patients or visits.

Like all screening or assessment instruments, AUDIT-C is a structured aid — not a diagnostic test in isolation. Results should be interpreted alongside history, examination, and clinical context. Where a score crosses an actionable threshold, the next step is typically a more detailed clinical evaluation rather than a definitive diagnosis.

AUDIT-C psychometric properties

  • Internal consistency. Cronbach's α = 0.8 — acceptable to good.
  • Test–retest reliability. r = 0.78 — stable across repeated administrations.
  • Validity. Sensitivity 95% for heavy drinking and active alcohol use disorders.

Psychometric figures are drawn from the validation literature and may vary across clinical populations and translations.

Limitations & common pitfalls

  • Sum 3 items scored 0-4 (or 0-12 depending on gender for item 3). Total range 0-12. Women >= 4, Men >= 5 = hazardous.
  • Screening, not diagnosis. A positive AUDIT-C score is not equivalent to a diagnosis. Confirmatory clinical evaluation is required before treatment decisions.
  • Population calibration. Cutoffs were derived from specific validation populations. Performance can differ in other age groups, languages, comorbid conditions, or clinical settings.
  • Self-report bias. Patient-reported instruments are subject to under- and over-reporting; clinical context is essential.

Frequently asked questions about AUDIT-C

What does AUDIT-C measure?

AUDIT-C (Alcohol Use Disorders Identification Test-Concise) is a validated instrument that assesses AUDIT for quick alcohol screening in clinical settings. It is designed for clinicians, medical students, evidence-synthesis researchers. It takes about 1 min to complete, and the 3 items are summed to produce a total score.

How long does AUDIT-C take to complete?

AUDIT-C typically takes about 1 min to administer. The exact duration varies a little depending on whether it is self-administered or completed with a clinician. Its brief format is part of what makes it practical for routine clinical use and for repeat administration when tracking change over time.

How many items are on AUDIT-C?

AUDIT-C contains 3 items. Item scores are summed to produce the total. Scores run from 0 to 12, grouped into 3 interpretation bands from ‘Low risk’ up to ‘Hazardous (men)’.

What is a high AUDIT-C score?

On the AUDIT-C (total score 0–12), scores of 5–12 fall in the ‘Hazardous (men)’ band — interpreted as: Brief intervention. The full ladder is: 0–3 — Low risk: None.; 4–5 — Hazardous (women): Brief intervention.; 5–12 — Hazardous (men): Brief intervention. A higher score reflects a more severe presentation, and crossing a cutoff is a prompt to step up assessment or care — but these bands are screening thresholds, not diagnoses.

What is a low AUDIT-C score?

Scores of 0–3 fall in the ‘Low risk’ band: None. A low score is reassuring but does not by itself rule out the underlying condition — if symptoms or clinical suspicion persist, reassess over time or use a complementary evaluation rather than relying on the number alone.

How reliable is AUDIT-C?

AUDIT-C has reported Cronbach's α of 0.8 in validation samples and test–retest reliability of 0.78. Sensitivity 95% for heavy drinking and active alcohol use disorders. In practical terms, a Cronbach’s α around 0.8 indicates good internal consistency (the items hang together well), and a test–retest reliability of 0.78 means scores are well when the scale is repeated without real clinical change.

Is AUDIT-C free to use?

AUDIT-C is free to use with attribution — clinical, educational, and research use is permitted provided the original source is credited. If you publish results or reproduce the scale, include the citation to the source paper.

What is the source paper for AUDIT-C?

Bush, K., Kivlahan, D. R., McDonell, M. B., Fihn, S. D., & Bradley, K. A. (1998). The AUDIT alcohol consumption questions. Archives of Internal Medicine, 158(16), 1789-1795. This is the original publication for the AUDIT-C — the reference to cite when reporting results.

Can AUDIT-C replace clinical judgment?

No. AUDIT-C is a structured assessment aid, not a diagnostic test. Its proper role is clinical assessment of AUDIT for quick alcohol screening in clinical settings — it standardizes assessment and flags who needs closer evaluation, but the result must be weighed alongside history, examination, and clinical context. When a score conflicts with your clinical impression, clinical judgment takes precedence; treatment decisions should never rest on a screening score alone.
