Clinical context: when NDI is used

The instrument's primary construct — self-reported neck-pain functional disability — is operationalized through a fixed set of items, each with a defined response format. This standardisation is what allows NDI scores to be compared meaningfully across clinicians, sites, and studies.

Like all screening or assessment instruments, NDI is a structured aid — not a diagnostic test in isolation. Results should be interpreted alongside history, examination, and clinical context. Where a score crosses an actionable threshold, the next step is typically a more detailed clinical evaluation rather than a definitive diagnosis.

How NDI is scored

NDI uses simple summation: each item's selected response is converted to a numeric value, and the values are added to produce a total score. Reverse-scored items are inverted before summation.

Scoring notes: Multiply raw score by 2 to get the conventional NDI percentage.

The cutoffs below are drawn from the published validation literature. Always interpret in clinical context.

Limitations & common pitfalls

  • Multiply raw score by 2 to get the conventional NDI percentage.
  • Screening, not diagnosis. A positive NDI score is not equivalent to a diagnosis. Confirmatory clinical evaluation is required before treatment decisions.
  • Population calibration. Cutoffs were derived from specific validation populations. Performance can differ in other age groups, languages, comorbid conditions, or clinical settings.
  • Self-report bias. Patient-reported instruments are subject to under- and over-reporting; clinical context is essential.
  • Comprehension and literacy. Item phrasing assumes baseline reading comprehension; consider clinician-administered alternatives where literacy or language barriers exist.

Frequently asked questions about NDI

What does NDI measure?

NDI (Neck Disability Index) is a validated instrument that assesses self-reported neck-pain functional disability. It is designed for clinicians, medical students, evidence-synthesis researchers. The 10 items are summed to produce a total score.

How many items are on NDI?

NDI contains 10 items. Item scores are summed to produce the total. Scores run from 0 to 50, grouped into 5 interpretation bands from ‘No disability (0–8%)’ up to ‘Complete disability (70–100%)’.

What is a high NDI score?

On the NDI (total score 0–50), scores of 35–50 fall in the ‘Complete disability (70–100%)’ band — interpreted as: Bed-bound or exaggerating; thorough evaluation. The full ladder is: 0–4 — No disability (0–8%): Pain has minimal interference with activities.; 5–14 — Mild disability (10–28%): Pain moderately affects daily activities; consider conservative care.; 15–24 — Moderate disability (30–48%): Pain interferes substantially; structured rehabilitation indicated.; 25–34 — Severe disability (50–68%): Major impairment; multidisciplinary management.; 35–50 — Complete disability (70–100%): Bed-bound or exaggerating; thorough evaluation. A higher score reflects a more severe presentation, and crossing a cutoff is a prompt to step up assessment or care — but these bands are screening thresholds, not diagnoses.

What is a low NDI score?

Scores of 0–4 fall in the ‘No disability (0–8%)’ band: Pain has minimal interference with activities. A low score is reassuring but does not by itself rule out the underlying condition — if symptoms or clinical suspicion persist, reassess over time or use a complementary evaluation rather than relying on the number alone.

Is NDI free to use?

NDI is free to use with attribution — clinical, educational, and research use is permitted provided the original source is credited. If you publish results or reproduce the scale, include the citation to the source paper.

What is the source paper for NDI?

Vernon H, Mior S. The Neck Disability Index. J Manipulative Physiol Ther. 1991;14(7):409-415. This is the original publication for the NDI — the reference to cite when reporting results.

Can NDI replace clinical judgment?

No. NDI is a structured assessment aid, not a diagnostic test. Its proper role is clinical assessment of self-reported neck-pain functional disability — it standardizes assessment and flags who needs closer evaluation, but the result must be weighed alongside history, examination, and clinical context. When a score conflicts with your clinical impression, clinical judgment takes precedence; treatment decisions should never rest on a screening score alone.
