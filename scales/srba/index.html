Clinical context: when SRBA is used

20-item measure of student attitudes toward research and research self-efficacy. SRBA is part of standard practice in this setting because it provides a structured, replicable assessment that can be tracked over time and compared across patients or visits.

Like all screening or assessment instruments, SRBA is a structured aid — not a diagnostic test in isolation. Results should be interpreted alongside history, examination, and clinical context. Where a score crosses an actionable threshold, the next step is typically a more detailed clinical evaluation rather than a definitive diagnosis.

SRBA psychometric properties

  • Internal consistency. Cronbach's α = 0.82 — acceptable to good.
  • Test–retest reliability. r = 0.75 — stable across repeated administrations.
  • Validity. Used in health sciences education research.

Psychometric figures are drawn from the validation literature and may vary across clinical populations and translations.

Limitations & common pitfalls

  • Sum all 20 items scored 1-5. Total range 20-100. Higher scores = more positive research attitudes.
  • Screening, not diagnosis. A positive SRBA score is not equivalent to a diagnosis. Confirmatory clinical evaluation is required before treatment decisions.
  • Population calibration. Cutoffs were derived from specific validation populations. Performance can differ in other age groups, languages, comorbid conditions, or clinical settings.
  • Self-report bias. Patient-reported instruments are subject to under- and over-reporting; clinical context is essential.
